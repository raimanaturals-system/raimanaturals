02The Signature Botanical

Aquilaria malaccensis, also known as Hindi Oud.

The tree spends most of its life as an ordinary evergreen. Resin formation begins only when its defences are triggered — a slow biological response that saturates the heartwood with the dark, dense compounds the trade calls oud. Indian material carries its own name in that trade: Hindi.

Resin formation is rare in the wild and never uniform. That scarcity built oud's reputation — and it built the two problems every serious buyer now has to solve before anything else: legality, and adulteration.

Every tree, a permit.
Every lot, a chromatogram.

We source only from sustainable plantations that induce resin formation by natural inoculation — no synthetic accelerants, no shortcuts that flatter a price list and fail a laboratory. The trees are planted, tended and harvested on a managed cycle, every consignment is traceable back to the plantation and the harvest window it came from, and every gram is fully CITES-compliant.

Oud is among the most adulterated materials in perfumery, routinely cut with sandalwood, vetiver, plasticisers or outright synthetic reconstruction and sold as the real thing. A full GC-MS profile is the only honest answer to that, and ours travels with the material rather than sitting in a drawer.

03Terroir

The eastern rim of the Himalaya.

Tripura is a country of ridges. Five hill ranges run north to south across the state, rising as they go east — from the low Baramura in the west to the Jampui on the Mizoram border, where Betlingchhip reaches 975 metres. Between each range lies a valley roughly twenty kilometres wide, and it is in these sheltered corridors, on red-and-yellow soils under five months of monsoon, that the aromatic crops grow.

Nearly three-quarters of the state is still under forest — semi-evergreen and moist deciduous, threaded with bamboo. The Tropic of Cancer crosses it. Tripura is one of the four states holding India’s cultivated Aquilaria, and the gondhoraj’s native belt runs directly across these same hills.

This is not a crop we are introducing. It is a crop we are finally extracting where it already belongs.

We take our name from the Raima, the river that gathers Tripura’s high valleys and meets the Sarma at Dumboor — the heart of the country we work in.

04Craft

Sourced, distilled, documented.

i.

Source

Plantations chosen on how they induce resin, not how fast. Naturally inoculated Aquilaria, planted stock, papers in order before a kilo moves.

ii.

Grade

Wood is assessed for resin content before it is bought. Sinking grade, floating grade and everything between behave differently in the still — and are priced as if they do not.

iii.

Distil

Chipped, soaked, then hydro-distilled slowly over days. Long, low, patient runs. Nothing added, nothing accelerated.

iv.

Document

Every lot ships with its provenance: plantation, harvest window, CITES permit, GC-MS trace. If we cannot document it, we do not sell it.

05The Founding Years

We are early. Deliberately.

A provenance house is built in the right order — the ground first, then the still, then the name on the bottle. Here is exactly where we stand.

Now

Next

Pilot distillations from graded plantation wood; evaluation lots characterised by GC-MS and allocated to founding partners.

Then

Commercial lots of Hindi oud — single origin, plantation-traced, permitted and profiled, and priced on what the chromatogram says rather than what the story does.
